# Section 8 — This is the organizational impact (summary)

Flow from Section 7: *"The process now has headroom. What is that headroom worth to HiLegal?"* Operational gains translated into business outcomes — with every connecting assumption stated. The story closes: the video process stops competing with the legal LLM and starts feeding it.

## The impact map (gain → mechanism → outcome)

| # | Operational gain | Connecting assumption | Business outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Capacity ~19 → ~190/mo, same two producers | Demand exceeds 19/mo and grows with subscribers [E] | **The paid service can scale** to every tier without hiring — video stops being the reason to say "no" |
| 2 | Cost ¥940 → ¥180/video | Typical customer ~2 of 10 videos [E] | **Typical customer flips positive**: −¥1,160 → **+¥360**/month; volume burn −¥150–195k/yr → ≈ break-even [E] |
| 3 | Human time 10 h → 1 h; ~4 h/mo team load at 20 videos | Saved payroll funds the core, not overhead [E — management decision] | **≈ ¥270k/yr production payroll released to the core** — money and attention, not video-maker hours, fund the LLM |

| 6 | SLA machinery + analytics (Iter 1/3) | Weekly dashboard review (PDCA) | **Management capability**: per-step cycle times, SLA compliance, engagement per video — the operations habit HiLegal lacks [C] |

## Three impact stories (pick per audience)

1. **Money (finance):** unit economics flip + payroll released + no hiring to scale. Annual swing ≈ −¥150–195k burn → ≈ break-even, +¥360/typical customer-month, plus ≈ ¥270k/yr redirected — before any revenue growth [E].
2. **Growth (commercial):** "video included, delivered in 48 h" is a subscription-selling feature; capacity no longer caps pricing/packaging experiments.
3. **Strategy (leadership — our framing):** the moat is currently maintained by hand, one video at a time. After three iterations it's maintained by a pipeline — and the pipeline *generates* fine-tuning data. **Operations stops taxing the strategy and starts compounding it.**
