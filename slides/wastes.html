TIME: 0:50. MAIN MESSAGE: Three wastes dominate. Waiting makes orders slow, overprocessing eats the team's hours, and rework makes both worse. Each one traces back to a root cause we can design out. SPEAKING POINTS: (1) TIMWOODS: we keep only the three wastes our evidence supports. Waiting: intake queue of 3–7 days and the three lawyer decisions; work is only about 6% of the lead time. Overprocessing: scripts drafted by hand and only then refined with AI, audio and video generated separately and synchronized by hand (2–3 h), plus ~2 h of manual platform adaptation; together sync and adaptation are about 40–45% of all labour (estimate). Defects and rework: at least four brief iterations, about 90% of videos loop at least once (estimate). (2) Five Whys, one root cause per waste: Waiting comes from unstructured intake (free-form requests, format settled only after drafts, so variety is absorbed manually). Overprocessing comes from a fragmented, manual toolchain with no structured data from the brief onwards, so nothing downstream can be automated. Rework comes from unstructured approvals and feedback (vague comments, producer guesses, rejected again, no approval workflow). (3) Theory of Constraints: what limits output is the team's total work content per video plus the rework it carries; the largest single manual block is A/V sync, but the longest task is not automatically the bottleneck. Lead time is gated by queues, not by capacity: faster approvals alone will not raise output, and automating sync alone will barely shorten lead time. That is why we need a chain of iterations, not one fix. CALCULATIONS (Appendix A): the stated ceiling 35–40 equals 100% of paid hours (2 × 176 h ÷ 10 h = 35.2), so there is no slack; at a realistic 60% utilisation capacity is ~19 per month, already at today's output of 15–20 (lecture Session_1.2, slide 05: utilisation = demand ÷ capacity). Queue model: μ ≈ 23.5 per month and demand 15–20 give utilisation 0.64–0.85 and an expected intake wait of 2.3–7.5 days, which reproduces the reported 3–7 days. LITTLE'S LAW (plausibility only): WIP = throughput × flow time = 15–20 per month × 0.47–0.93 months ≈ 7–19 orders in progress (lecture Session_2.1, slide 3; assumes a stable system; ranges are not paired observations).
