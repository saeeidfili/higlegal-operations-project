SPEAKER: Joel Wirth. TIME: 1:00. KEY MESSAGE: How we roll it out: measure first, then the three iterations one after the other, each tested and gated, then keep it running. Cheapest risk first. SAY: (1) "How do we get there? Standardize first, automate second. Iteration 1 needs no new AI at all." (2) Baseline row: "Two weeks to measure today's numbers, so every later change can be proven." (3) Gold frame, one line each: "Then the three iterations: first we standardize briefs and approvals, then the legal LLM drafts the scripts, then one render produces the whole video." (4) "Each iteration is tested on 10 pilot videos. Only if it meets its targets, the 'move on if' line, does the next one start. Then we keep it running." (5) Circle: "Every step runs the same loop: Plan, Do, Check, Act." (6) Bottom left: "The investment is about 105 to 180 thousand yuan once, paid back in about 5 to 8 months. We hand over when HiLegal runs the weekly review alone for three weeks." (7) Red box: "And three stop signals: if videos get slow, if lawyers stop approving on time, or if any video lacks the lawyer's consent, we stop and fix it first." TRANSITION: "To sum up." BACKGROUND: - Iteration 1, Standardize (weeks 3–6): structured request form with previews, automatic completeness check instead of back-and-forth, brief auto-filled from a template, lawyer approval with a 48 h deadline. Removes the intake queue and the clarification loops. No new AI. - Move on if (Iteration 1): lead ≤ 12 d = request to delivered video in at most 12 calendar days. Rework ≤ 60% = at most 6 of 10 pilot videos need any redo. ≥ 35% right first time = at least 35% pass every check without a single loop. - Iteration 2, Automate scripting (weeks 7–10): HiLegal's fine-tuned legal LLM writes the first draft from the template and the lawyer's profile; a producer edits and checks it. Every edit becomes training data. Removes the script loops. - Move on if (Iteration 2): lead ≤ 5 d. ≤ 20% of script edited = the producer changes at most a fifth of the words in the AI draft, so the draft is mostly usable. No legal errors = zero compliance incidents in the pilot videos. - Iteration 3, Integrate production (weeks 11–14): one avatar render makes voice, lip-sync, video and all three formats; a person checks every render (~20 min). Consent on file before every render; the lawyer can object within 24 h after delivery. Removes 2–3 h of manual sync and ~2 h of format adaptation. - Move on if (Iteration 3): lead ≤ 2 d. ≥ 90% usable renders = at least 9 of 10 renders pass the check on the first try. Full consent = a signed consent for the lawyer's face and voice exists for every video. IF ASKED: - Why this order: automating production before standardizing intake produces the wrong video faster. Each step needs the structured data of the step before. - Saeed's project plan uses phases: P0 baseline, P1 = Iteration 1, P2a = Iteration 2, P2b = Iteration 3, P3 sustain. P2 is split because the script engine and the render pipeline are two separate technical builds. On the slide we show the iterations directly. - "Stop the line" comes from lean production: anyone may stop the line when quality is at risk. - Over 10% of videos take more than 5 days: the P90 lead time is above 5 days, so the new process is not delivering as planned. - Under 85% of approvals on time: the 48 h approval SLA is missed too often, so the flow stalls at the lawyer. - Any video without lawyer consent: each avatar video uses the lawyer's face and voice; one missing consent is a legal risk, so the line stops at once. - Payback: ¥105–180k ÷ ¥760 saved per video ≈ 140–240 videos, about 5–8 months at 20–35 videos a month. - Investment = forms and approvals ¥35–65k + templates and prompts ¥25–40k + render and LLM integration ¥45–75k. Calculations: Appendix B. - Roles: production lead owns the weekly PDCA review, one engineer at about 30% in weeks 7–14, COO or founder decides go or no-go.
