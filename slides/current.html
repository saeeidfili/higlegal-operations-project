TIME: 1:30. MAIN MESSAGE: One video order spends about ten hours being worked on and two to four weeks in total; almost all of its lead time is waiting in queues and at approval gates. SPEAKING POINTS: (1) Flow unit: one video order. Boundary: from the video request to "video sent for publishing". We keep this boundary on every slide. (2) Walk the order through the map: request, intake queue (3–7 days), content brief, clarification gate that can send the order back to the queue, script drafting, approval gate that can send it back to drafting, AI refinement, final script, separate video and audio generation, manual audio-visual synchronization (2–3 h), final approval that can send it back to the final script, and delivery. (3) White boxes are active work, amber boxes are waiting, dark dashed lines are rework loops. (4) Bottom bar: roughly 10 h of active work inside 14–28 days. CALCULATION: 10 h ÷ (14 × 24 h) = 3.0%; 10 h ÷ (28 × 24 h) = 1.5%. In business hours (10–20 business days × 8 h = 80–160 h) the share is about 6–13%. Either way, waiting dominates.
