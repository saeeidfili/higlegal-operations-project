TIME: 1:30. MAIN MESSAGE: One video order spends about ten hours being worked on and two to four weeks in total; almost all of its lead time is waiting in queues and at the lawyer's decisions. SPEAKING POINTS: (1) Flow unit: one video order. Boundary: from the video request to the finished video delivered to the lawyer; publishing on social media stays with the lawyer. (2) Walk the order through the twelve steps: request (1), intake queue of 3–7 days (2), clarification with the lawyer (3), which sends unclear requests back to the queue, content brief (4), script drafting (5) and AI refinement (6), script approval (7), which sends rejected scripts back to drafting, approved final script (8), video generation (9), then audio generation (10), manual audio-visual synchronization of 2–3 h (11), final approval (12a), which sends changes back to the final script, and platform adaptation of about 2 h (12b) before delivery. (3) Notation: rectangles are activities, triangles are queues where the order waits, diamonds are the lawyer's decisions, dashed arrows are the three rework loops. (4) Bottom bar: roughly 10 h of active work inside 14–28 days. CALCULATION: 10 h ÷ (14 × 24 h) = 3.0%; 10 h ÷ (28 × 24 h) = 1.5%. In business hours (10–20 business days × 8 h = 80–160 h) the share is about 6–13%. Either way, waiting dominates. DATA STATUS: step sequence and the three loops as confirmed with HiLegal (Sep 2026), numbered as in the project's process summary. 3–7 days, 2–3 h, ~2 h, ~10 h and 14–28 days are company-reported estimates. Tools: ChatGPT for text, Seedance for video, a separate TTS tool for audio. Platform adaptation (~2 h, company-reported) is HiLegal work inside the ~10 h; publishing itself is done by the lawyer.
