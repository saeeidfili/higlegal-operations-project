TIME: 1:30. MAIN MESSAGE: We stress-test the baseline and the improved process with three changes. Automation makes the process robust to demand; lead time stays hostage to lawyer response time and rework rounds. WHY NO SIMULATION: we lack distributions for arrivals, task times and approval delays, so a numerical simulation would only repeat our assumptions with false precision. We use a transparent scenario comparison instead. The moving dot on slides 4 and 9 illustrates flow; it validates nothing. PANEL A — DEMAND: utilisation = demand ÷ capacity (lecture Session_1.2 slide 05). Demand levels 20, 40, 80 per month are illustrative, not forecasts. Baseline capacity 35–40 (reported ceiling); Iteration 3 44–57 (calculated); target-based 350–400 (calculated, only if under 1 h per video). E.g. 40 ÷ 35 = 114%, 40 ÷ 40 = 100%. Above 100% the queue grows without limit. Even below 100%, queueing theory shows waits rising sharply as utilisation approaches 100% (Session-8 queueing slides 03, 09, 11: congestion factor ρ/(1−ρ) is 3 at 75%, 9 at 90%). We do not apply M/M/1 or P-K numerically because their assumptions (Poisson arrivals, one server, steady state) are not verified here. PANEL B — APPROVAL DELAY: lead time = HiLegal-internal time + lawyer wait × number of approval gates. Internal time assumed ≤ 1 business day (the proposal's target implies this; render queue time is not measured). 48 h SLA treated as 2 business days. One gate = proposal's eight-step design; two gates = our Iteration 3 with the final video approval kept. Examples: 1 + 0.5 = 1.5; 1 + 2 = 3; 1 + 2 × 2 = 5; 1 + 2 × 5 = 11. PANEL C — REWORK: one gate, each round waits the full 48 h SLA: lead time = 1 + 2 × rounds → 3, 5, 9 BD. Four rounds mirrors the proposal's worst case (four iterations per gate). Baseline: a failed clarification returns to the intake queue (Fig. 1); if it re-waits the full 3–7 days, each extra loop adds 3–7 days. UNITS: baseline 14–28 calendar days ≈ 10–20 business days (× 5/7). TAKEAWAY FOR HILEGAL: monitor lawyer response time as its own metric; it is outside HiLegal's direct control but decides the lead time.
