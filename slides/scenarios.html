TIME: 1:30. MAIN MESSAGE: We stress-test the baseline and the improved process with three changes. Automation makes the process robust to demand; lead time stays hostage to lawyer response time and rework rounds. MODELS: our baseline queue model (capacity μ ≈ 23.5 per month, calibrated to the reported 3–7-day intake wait) and our rework simulation (λ = 17.5 per month: mean lead 20.7 d, P90 32 d; heavy rework 28.8 d, P90 47.8 d, calendar days) describe today's process only. The improved processes are not simulated; their panels use transparent arithmetic. The moving dot on slides 6 and 11 illustrates flow; it validates nothing. PANEL A — DEMAND: utilisation = demand ÷ capacity (lecture Session_1.2 slide 05). Demand levels 20, 40, 80 per month are illustrative, not forecasts. Baseline capacity ~19 (KPI frame, 60% utilisation) to ~24 (simulation, 67% dedication); Iteration 2 ~33 and Iteration 3 ~190 (calculated from targets). E.g. 40 ÷ 19 = 211%, 40 ÷ 33 = 121%. Above 100% the queue grows without limit; below 100%, waits rise sharply as utilisation approaches 100% (Session-8 slides 03, 09, 11: congestion ρ/(1−ρ) is 3 at 75%, 9 at 90%). Queue model check: at 20 requests per month ρ = 0.85 and the intake wait is ~7.5 days, the top of the reported 3–7 days. PANEL B — APPROVAL DELAY: lead time = HiLegal-internal time + lawyer wait × number of approval gates. Internal time assumed ≤ 1 business day (the proposal's target implies this; render queue time is not measured). 48 h SLA treated as 2 business days. One gate = proposal's eight-step design (final approval as a parallel objection window); two gates = our Iteration 3 with a serial final video approval kept. Examples: 1 + 0.5 = 1.5; 1 + 2 = 3; 1 + 2 × 2 = 5; 1 + 2 × 5 = 11. PANEL C — REWORK: one gate, each round waits the full 48 h SLA: lead time = 1 + 2 × rounds → 3, 5, 9 BD. Four rounds mirrors the proposal's worst case (four iterations per gate). Baseline: our rework simulation gives 20.7 d mean lead time (P90 32 d) and 28.8 d (P90 47.8 d) with heavy rework; a stress case, not a forecast. UNITS: baseline 14–28 calendar days ≈ 10–20 business days (× 5/7). TAKEAWAY FOR HILEGAL: monitor lawyer response time as its own metric; it is outside HiLegal's direct control but decides the lead time.
