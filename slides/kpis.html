SPEAKER: Saeed Filizadeh. TIME: 0:45. MAIN MESSAGE: Before any solution we fix how success is measured: two main KPIs, one for HiLegal (cost per video) and one for the lawyer (lead time). Every iteration has to move both; four supporting KPIs explain why. SPEAKING POINTS: (1) Cost per video, about ¥940 (estimate): every manual minute is recurring cost, so it answers whether the service stops losing money. (2) Lead time, 14–28 days (company-reported, about 21 days in the model): the lawyer's campaign pays for every queued day, so it answers whether the lawyer stops waiting. (3) The two are independent: a change can make videos cheaper but slower, or faster but more expensive. We only accept changes that move both. (4) Supporting KPIs: human time (~10 h, company-reported), throughput (15–20 a month reported; capacity about 19 at 60% effective use, model estimate), rework rate (~90%, estimate) and first-pass quality (about 10%, the share accepted first time at every gate; the complement of the rework estimate, not a measured QC result). (5) Plan: simulate first, then pilot. A pilot passes only if both main KPIs hit their target and no supporting KPI gets worse. DEFINITIONS: lead time = request received to delivery of the finished video, in calendar days. Throughput = videos completed per month; capacity = maximum sustainable output; higher capacity raises throughput only when demand exists. Rework rate counts orders with at least one return; one order with four rounds counts once. DATA STATUS: company-reported: lead time, ~10 h, 15–20 per month. Estimates: ¥940, capacity ~19, rework ~90%, first-pass ~10%. The pilot must replace estimates with observations. The same six KPIs are used on the iteration slides.
