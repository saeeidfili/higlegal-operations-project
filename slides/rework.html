TIME: 0:40. MAIN MESSAGE: Rework looks like extra work, but it is mostly extra waiting. In our model, rework adds 84 minutes of work and almost eleven days of lead time. SPEAKING POINTS: (1) Left: the same order with and without rework loops. Work rises only from 516 to 600 minutes (+16%), lead time almost doubles from 11.0 to 21.8 days (+98%). Removing rework would cut lead time by about half but work by only 14%. (2) Right: why. Unstructured briefs and approvals cause repeated loops at the three lawyer decisions. Every loop is a few minutes of work but a fresh wait at the gate and another turn in the team's queue. More orders sit half-finished in the system: work in progress rises from 6.2 to 12.7 videos. By Little's Law, lead time = WIP ÷ throughput, so lead time rises with it. (3) Rework also lowers capacity: the modelled ceiling drops from 27.4 to 23.6 videos per month. Reducing rework is the largest single lead-time lever, which is why Iteration 1 starts at the brief and the approvals. DATA STATUS: modelled results.
